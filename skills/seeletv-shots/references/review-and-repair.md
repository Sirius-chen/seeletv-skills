# Prompt and Take Review

## Before delivering a prompt

Check the result against the request and supplied scene:

- Important actions, exact required lines, speakers, information reveals, and ending remain intact.
- Every shot identifies its visible participants, readable spatial relationships, camera start, intended movement or hold, performance, and endpoint at a useful level of detail.
- Timing fits the action and dialogue; intervals and cuts are coherent; simultaneous camera instructions do not contradict each other.
- Hands, prop ownership, eye-lines, screen travel, costume and location states carry across the sequence. Deliberate ellipses, repeats, or disorientation are identified.
- Start frames do not contain future results. Independent clip prompts contain their own needed setup.
- References actually exist or are explicitly pending; their roles and order are understandable. Provider-specific syntax and controls are verified or clearly left generic.

Check only relevant dimensions. An object insert need not invent a human performance, and a static shot need not acquire camera movement to satisfy a checklist.

## Inspect generated footage

Open the actual media when available. For a video, inspect enough of the movement and transitions to assess the requested criteria; a final frame alone cannot prove temporal stability or complete dialogue. If the environment cannot inspect video or sound, say what remains unverified rather than declaring the take passed.

Describe failures with time ranges and observable evidence: a face changes after a turn, a second object appears during a handoff, a line restarts after the cut, or the camera settles on the wrong subject. Technical success, file existence, and correct dimensions do not establish creative quality.

Distinguish an observation from a possible cause. Check mode mismatch, conflicting references, overloaded actions, insufficient time, unclear geography, unsupported control, and ordinary model variation before blaming the prompt. Do not claim knowledge of hidden model internals.

## Repair with a bounded change

Preserve usable performance, approved identities, settings, and reference roles. Choose the smallest change addressing the evidence: clarify a handoff, simplify the camera path, separate a reveal from its reaction, adjust timing within the requested scope, correct a reference, or propose a different mode. Recompile a clean prompt; do not append a history of failures.

A repair that changes required dialogue, story outcome, mode, cost, or agreed scope is a proposal until the user authorizes that change. A request to review or rewrite does not itself authorize another paid generation. For already authorized generation, honor the stated attempt and spending limits; absent a larger iteration allowance, stop after one targeted repair attempt if the same issue persists and report the remaining choice.

For a sequence, repair the failing shot or boundary first. Reassess downstream material that actually depends on the changed state, retaining unaffected accepted work. Deliver a usable revised prompt when requested, along with the specific remaining uncertainty; do not replace it with a diagnosis alone.
