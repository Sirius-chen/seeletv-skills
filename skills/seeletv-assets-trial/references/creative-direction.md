# Creative Direction

## Derive the tone

Start from the approved script interpretation and user preferences: genre, emotional temperature, realism, period, scale, pace, and central visual tension. Recommend one coherent direction, allowing alternatives only for a consequential unresolved choice. Do not choose a favorite film first and force the script to match it.

Support the chosen medium, including live action, animation, or stylization. Use references only when they clarify a decision. Name the borrowed dimension—lighting, color relationships, spatial pressure, texture, scale, or performance distance—and translate it into visible properties. An exact title is unnecessary when photographic or design language is clearer.

## Visual system

Specify enough to constrain later assets without pretending to plan every shot:

- **Image formation:** medium and relevant camera/lens or rendering characteristics, framing, aspect ratio, and motion treatment if applicable. Name equipment or frame rate only when useful to the actual workflow. Distinguish capture from finishing; digital capture with film emulation is different from film capture.
- **Lighting and exposure:** motivated sources, direction, softness, contrast, highlight behavior, shadow detail, and skin treatment. For stylized media, use the equivalent design language rather than imposing live-action physics.
- **Palette:** dominant and supporting color relationships, temperature, saturation, black level, midtones, skin protection, and meaningful accent colors. Supply exact color values only from an identified source or as explicitly proposed design swatches, not as extracted facts.
- **Texture and materials:** sharpness, grain or noise, bloom, atmospheric effects, and the behavior of skin, fabric, wood, metal, or other relevant surfaces. Describe visible outcomes rather than generic quality claims.
- **Continuity:** what remains stable across the project and which changes follow story states.

Distinguish final scene treatment from neutral asset documentation. Character and prop masters normally preserve intrinsic colors and readable materials under neutral light; dramatic scene lighting must not silently redefine them.

Deliver a short reusable style paragraph plus any decisions that need more explanation. Avoid contradictory capture systems, decorative jargon, and long equipment lists with no practical effect.

## Optional pacing analysis

Include only when requested or useful to a stated tone decision. Extract real changes such as threat escalation, decisions, failure, revelation, reversal, false safety, release, and aftermath. Use story order; add duration estimates only when supported.

Choose enough nodes to explain the rhythm. Do not force a relief beat into a story that lacks one. Tension and emotional impact may diverge: describe them separately when a single curve would obscure the distinction. Any numeric intensity is an editorial estimate, not a measurement or model prediction.

A node table or editable chart is normally sufficient. Label events consistently across the curve, turning points, and any music notes. Regenerate the analysis when relevant beats change; a wording-only edit does not necessarily change pacing. Image generation is not a prerequisite for confirming the brief.

## Optional sound and music principles

Include when relevant to the requested brief, without automatically generating audio or standalone music prompts.

Separate environmental sound, event-synchronous effects, nonverbal voice, dialogue performance, and music. Describe how space, perspective, texture, silence, and recurring sonic motifs support the story. Avoid assigning accents, vocal traits, or cultural conventions without evidence or a clearly labeled proposal.

For music, establish its dramatic role, a coherent instrument or timbre palette, rhythmic language, motif, density, register, dynamics, and deliberate silence. Music need not cover every scene. Recommendations should follow the genre and user intent rather than impose one universal cinematic taste.

## Approval and reuse

Present the direction with its consequential assumptions for approval. Combine script and tone confirmation when useful; do not create extra gates merely because these references are separate files. Record confirmed choices in the project brief. If a later change affects existing assets, assess those dependencies using the revision procedure in `asset-production.md`.
